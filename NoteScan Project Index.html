P01 · Domain discovery and value-driver analysis

Phase: A · Discover · Role: Senior domain analyst · Produces: value-driver taxonomy, hypothesis verdicts, open questions · Depends on: none

Purpose

Establish which attributes of a collectible make it more or less valuable, which of them can be read from a photograph, and which beliefs held by the owner are correct, unverified or wrong. Everything later (rules, extraction fields, reference data) is derived from this analysis.

Prompt

ROLE
You are a senior domain analyst for the {{COLLECTIBLE}} market with experience advising collectors and resellers.

CONTEXT
I am designing {{PRODUCT_NAME}}, a scanner for {{COLLECTIBLE}} ({{ISSUER_SCOPE}}) intended for {{USER_PERSONA}}.
I currently believe the following, and I want each belief tested rather than confirmed by default:
{{USER_HYPOTHESES}}

OBJECTIVE
Identify the attributes that drive collector value in this domain and decide which can be observed from a single photograph.

INPUTS
- The domain and scope stated above.
- My hypotheses, listed one per line.

CONSTRAINTS
- Classify every claim as Confirmed, Unverified or Incorrect. State your basis for each.
- Do not quote prices unless you can name the source and the date. If you cannot, write "no verified price".
- Identify common scam and misrepresentation patterns seen in this market.
- Do not give investment or legal advice.
- If you are answering from general knowledge and have not searched, say so explicitly.

DELIVERABLES
1. A taxonomy of value drivers in four groups: (a) identifiers and patterns, (b) issuer or authority attributes, (c) physical condition, (d) special issues and errors.
2. For each driver: why it matters, and whether it is observable from a photo (Yes, Partly, No).
3. A verdict table for each of my hypotheses: claim, verdict, basis.
4. A list of risks: scams, counterfeits, condition pitfalls.
5. A list of open questions that need research before specification.

QUALITY BAR
- Every driver appears in exactly one group.
- No unexplained jargon; define each term at first use.
- Uncertainty is stated, never smoothed over.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Markdown with the five deliverables as numbered headings. Tables for items 2 and 3.

Required inputs

  • Collectible class and the issuer scope.
  • Your hypotheses, even if you are unsure of them.

Acceptance checks

  • Each hypothesis has a verdict and a stated basis.
  • The observable-from-photo column exists for every driver.
  • At least one scam pattern is named.
  • The response says whether any search was performed.

Pitfalls

  • A model that agrees with every hypothesis. Require the Incorrect and Unverified categories to be considered explicitly.
  • Prices presented without a source. Reject and re-run with the constraint restated.

Adapting to other collectibles

  • Coins: include mint marks, mintage figures, die varieties, metal, and grading scale in group (a) to (c). Cleaning damage is a condition pitfall.
  • Stamps: include perforation, watermark, shade, centering, gum and cancellation. Reperforation and forgeries belong in the risk list.

P02 · Market and competitor scan

Phase: A · Discover · Role: Product researcher · Produces: competitor matrix, gap analysis, differentiation hypotheses · Depends on: P01

Purpose

Test the assumption that no comparable product exists before investing in the build. The result decides how much to invest in the differentiating parts (usually rules and reference data) and how little in commodity parts.

Prompt

ROLE
You are a product researcher. You verify claims with live sources and you report absence of evidence as absence of evidence, not as proof.

CONTEXT
{{PRODUCT_NAME}} will scan {{COLLECTIBLE}} ({{ISSUER_SCOPE}}) for {{USER_PERSONA}}. The value drivers identified earlier are:
{{VALUE_DRIVERS_FROM_P01}}

OBJECTIVE
Determine what products and services already address this need and where the gaps are.

INPUTS
- The value drivers above.
- Platforms to check: Google Play, Apple App Store, the open web, collector communities, auction-house tools.

CONSTRAINTS
- Use web search. Record the query and date of each search.
- For each product: name, platform, what it identifies, whether it flags valuable items, pricing model, last update if visible, and the source link.
- Do not state that "nothing exists" unless you ran at least eight distinct searches. Otherwise write "not found in the searches performed".
- Separate facts from marketing claims. Quote at most one short phrase per source.
- Mark unverifiable items "Not verified".

DELIVERABLES
1. Competitor matrix: product, platform, coverage of this domain, extraction method, flags valuable items (Yes, No, Unclear), price model, source.
2. Gap analysis: needs from the value drivers that no product serves well.
3. Three differentiation hypotheses, each with the evidence that would confirm or reject it.
4. A recommendation: build, adapt an existing product, or stop. State the reasoning.
5. A list of searches performed.

QUALITY BAR
- Every row has a link.
- The recommendation follows from the matrix.
- Limits of the research are stated.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Markdown. Tables for deliverables 1 and 2. A "Sources" list at the end.

Required inputs

  • Output of P01 (value drivers).
  • Web search must be available to the model.

00 · Constitution

Non-negotiable principles for NoteScan. Every spec, task and code change must respect these. If a change breaks one, change the constitution first, on purpose, and record why in 08-decisions.md.

Principles

  1. AI reads, code decides. The AI model only extracts facts from a photo (serial, denomination, signature, series, condition). Scoring and flagging are deterministic code that anyone can read and test.
  2. The human confirms the serial. No note is scored or saved from an unreviewed AI reading. The serial field is always editable before saving.
  3. A score is not a price. The app must never present a pattern score as a market value. Any screen showing a score must carry the reminder to check sold prices.
  4. Never guess. If a digit is ambiguous the AI must lower its confidence and say so, not fill it in. The UI shows that confidence.
  5. Secrets stay on the server. The Anthropic API key is never sent to the browser or committed to the repo.
  6. Private by default. Collection data stays on the user's device (localStorage) unless the user exports it.
  7. Cheap to run, safe to leak. Every scan costs money, so the server enforces a passcode (when set) and a per-IP hourly limit.
  8. Honest about unverified data. Reference data that has not been verified against a source is labelled "not verified", never presented as fact.
  9. Spec first. Behaviour is described in specs/ before it is built. Code that has no spec is either specified retroactively or removed.

Definition of done

A task is done when: its acceptance criteria in 01-product-spec.md pass, relevant tests in 06-test-plan.md pass, the task is ticked in 05-tasks.md, and any decision taken is recorded in 08-decisions.md.

01 · Product spec

Problem

People who collect or flip Indian banknotes want to know quickly whether a note in hand is worth more than its face value. Today they compare serial patterns by memory, search forums, and often get fooled by viral "sell your 786 note for lakhs" scams.

Users

  • Hobby collector or flipper checking notes from bank withdrawals, shops or inherited stashes.
  • Single user per device. No accounts in v1.

Goals (v1)

  • G1. Photograph a note and get its serial number and basic details without typing.
  • G2. Flag fancy serial numbers and explain why each flag fired.
  • G3. Keep a sortable table of scanned notes and export it.
  • G4. Work as an installable web app on a phone.

Non-goals (v1)

  • Price estimates or valuations.
  • Signature and series rarity lookup (planned for v2, see 09-roadmap.md).
  • Error-note detection beyond what the AI mentions in free text.
  • Accounts, sync, marketplace, or authentication of genuine versus counterfeit notes.

User stories and acceptance criteria

US-1 Scan a note

As a collector I take a photo and the app reads the note.

  • AC-1.1 The user can take a photo with the phone camera or choose an existing image.
  • AC-1.2 The photo is shrunk in the browser (longest side 1600 px, JPEG 0.85) before upload.
  • AC-1.3 The app shows progress ("Reading note…") and a readable error if the scan fails.
  • AC-1.4 The server returns the fields defined in 03-scan-api-spec.md.

US-2 Review and correct

As a collector I can fix misread digits before anything is scored or saved.

  • AC-2.1 Serial, denomination, signature, series, condition and notes are editable.
  • AC-2.2 The AI's confidence in the serial digits is shown; below 80% the UI tells the user to double-check every digit.
  • AC-2.3 The verdict (tier, score, reasons) updates live as the serial is edited.
  • AC-2.4 If the image is not readable as a banknote the UI says so and still allows manual entry.

US-3 Spot fancy numbers

As a collector I see which patterns make a serial interesting.

  • AC-3.1 Patterns detected are exactly those listed in 02-rules-engine-spec.md.
  • AC-3.2 Every flag shows a label and a plain-English explanation.
  • AC-3.3 Score and tier follow the formula in 02-rules-engine-spec.md.
  • AC-3.4 The user can add watchlist numbers (for example 541) in Settings and notes containing them are flagged.
  • AC-3.5 A score is always accompanied by the "not a price" reminder.

US-4 Keep a collection

As a collector I save notes and review them later.

  • AC-4.1 Saved notes persist across page reloads on the same device.
  • AC-4.2 The table can be sorted by tier, serial, denomination, signature, series and condition.
  • AC-4.3 A note can be deleted; the whole collection can be cleared after a confirmation.
  • AC-4.4 The collection can be exported to CSV. Cells that begin with =, +, - or @ are neutralised so spreadsheets never run them as formulas.

US-5 Install and use on a phone

  • AC-5.1 The app has a web manifest and icons (192 and 512 px) and registers a service worker.
  • AC-5.2 The app shell opens offline; scanning itself needs a connection.
  • AC-5.3 The layout works on a phone-width screen with tables scrolling horizontally.

US-6 Protect the owner's wallet

  • AC-6.1 If APP_PASSCODE is set, /api/scan rejects requests without the matching x-app-passcode header with HTTP 401.
  • AC-6.2 Each IP is limited to MAX_SCANS_PER_HOUR scans per hour (default 40); the next scan returns HTTP 429.
  • AC-6.3 If ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is missing the endpoint returns a clear HTTP 500 message instead of crashing.

02 · Rules engine spec

Implemented in public/fancy.js. Runs in the browser and in Node. Pure functions, no network.

Input parsing

parseSerial(raw):

  1. Uppercase the input and drop every character except 0-9, A-Z, *, ★.
  2. star is true if the cleaned string contains * or ★.
  3. digits is the trailing run of digits, keeping at most the last 6.
  4. prefix is everything before that trailing run, with star symbols removed.

Example: 01A 124578 gives prefix 01A, digits 124578, star false.

analyze(rawSerial, { watchlist }) returns { serial, prefix, digits, tags[], score, tier, summary }.

If digits has fewer than 3 characters the tier is Unreadable, score 0, and no tags.

Endpoints

GET /api/health

Returns { "ok": true, "aiConfigured": boolean, "passcodeRequired": boolean }.

POST /api/scan

Request headers:

  • Content-Type: application/json

Request body (limit 12 MB):

{ "image": "<base64 or data URL>", "mediaType": "image/jpeg" }

mediaType must be image/jpeg, image/png or image/webp; anything else falls back to image/jpeg.

Success (200):

{ "result": { /* ScanResult, below */ } }

StatusWhenBody

# 03 · Scan API spec

Implemented in `server.js`.

## Endpoints

### `GET /api/health`
Returns `{ "ok": true, "aiConfigured": boolean, "passcodeRequired": boolean }`.

### `POST /api/scan`

Request headers:
- `Content-Type: application/json`
- `x-app-passcode: <string>` required only when the server has `APP_PASSCODE` set.

Request body (limit 12 MB):
```json
{ "image": "<base64 or data URL>", "mediaType": "image/jpeg" }
```
`mediaType` must be `image/jpeg`, `image/png` or `image/webp`; anything else falls back to `image/jpeg`.

Success (`200`):
```json
{ "result": { /* ScanResult, below */ } }
```

### Errors

| Status | When | Body |
|---|---|---|
| 400 | `image` missing or not a string | `{ "error": "Missing image." }` |
| 401 | passcode set and header wrong or absent | `{ "error": "Wrong or missing passcode." }` |
| 429 | IP exceeded `MAX_SCANS_PER_HOUR` | `{ "error": "Scan limit reached for this hour. Try again later." }` |
| 500 | no `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` | `{ "error": "Server has no ANTHROPIC_API_KEY set." }` |
| 502 | model call failed or reply had no valid JSON | `{ "error": "Could not read the note. Try a clearer, well-lit photo." }` |

The order of checks is: passcode, API key, rate limit, body validation.

## ScanResult

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `readable` | boolean | false if the image is not a banknote |
| `denomination` | number or null | rupees |
| `serial_full` | string or null | complete serial as printed, e.g. `5AB 123456` |
| `is_star` | boolean | star symbol in the number panel |
| `governor_signature` | string or null | governor name, null if unsure |
| `series_or_year` | string or null | printed year or series |
| `special_markings` | string[] | e.g. `HAJ inscription`, `pin holes`, `ink smear` |
| `condition_estimate` | `UNC` `AU` `XF` `VF` `F` `poor` or null | rough grade |
| `confidence` | number 0–1 | certainty about the serial digits |
| `notes` | string | short comment, at most about 25 words |

The server does not validate individual fields beyond parsing JSON. The client treats every field as optional and always lets the user correct it.

## Model prompt (contract)

```
You are reading a photo of an Indian banknote (Reserve Bank of India or older issue).
Return ONLY a JSON object, no other text, with exactly these keys:
{ readable, denomination, serial_full, is_star, governor_signature,
  series_or_year, special_markings, condition_estimate, confidence, notes }
Rules: Never guess digits you cannot see clearly. If a character is ambiguous,
lower confidence and mention it in notes.
If the image is not a banknote, set readable to false.
```
The full wording lives in `server.js` (`PROMPT`). Keep this spec and that constant in sync.

|---|---|---|

- Gulf notes use prefix `Z`, were made for use in the Persian Gulf countries and were demonetised in 1970 (Numista).
- Haj notes are far rarer than Gulf notes; do not price them alike.

ADR-001 · Installable web app (PWA) instead of a native app

  • Context: The owner wanted a mobile app to try quickly on a real phone. Options were a PWA, Android only, or iOS plus Android.
  • Decision: PWA. Chosen by the user from the offered options.
  • Consequences: One codebase for Android and iPhone, no store review, instant updates. Needs HTTPS hosting for install. Camera access goes through the file input with capture, not live video. A native app can reuse the server and fancy.js later.

ADR-002 · Cloud AI vision to read the note

  • Context: On-device OCR is free and offline but reads only digits reliably; it cannot identify signature, series, markings or condition. Manual entry is fastest to build but defeats the point.
  • Decision: Send a photo to a Claude vision model through our own server. Chosen by the user.
  • Consequences: Per-scan cost and an internet requirement. Higher accuracy and richer fields. Needs a server to hold the key.

ADR-003 · Rules in code, not in the model

  • Context: The model could also judge "is this valuable". That would be opaque, inconsistent and unverifiable.
  • Decision: The model only extracts facts. Scoring is deterministic code (spec 02) with tests.
  • Consequences: Reproducible results, easy tuning, tests possible. The model's mistakes affect only the serial reading, which the user corrects.

ADR-004 · Mandatory human review of the serial

  • Context: A single misread digit changes the verdict completely.
  • Decision: The review step is part of the flow; confidence is shown; the serial is editable and the verdict recalculates live.
  • Consequences: One extra tap per note. Far fewer wrong flags.

ADR-005 · Version 1 scope: fancy-number detector and collection table

  • Context: The user chose these two from four options. Value estimates and signature lookup were not selected.
  • Decision: Ship those two. Signature and series lookup moves to v2. No price estimates until verified sold data exists.
  • Consequences: The app can say "786, ladder" but not yet "Ghosh signature". This is the main gap versus the original idea.

ADR-006 · Local storage and a shared passcode

  • Context: Single user, personal use, no budget for accounts.
  • Decision: Collection in localStorage; one optional shared passcode and a per-IP hourly cap protect the API.
  • Consequences: Data lives on one device and is lost if browser data is cleared (hence CSV export). Not safe for public release without real authentication.

ADR-007 · SDK dependency set to latest

  • Context: The build sandbox could not reach the npm registry, so no version could be verified. A guessed version number could break installation.
  • Decision: @anthropic-ai/sdk is latest in package.json.
  • Consequences: Installs always work, but builds are not reproducible. Once the install succeeds on a real machine, pin the installed version and commit the lockfile.

  • Context: Research produced useful but partly unverified signature and price information, and one earlier mistake (Osborne Smith).
  • Decision: Keep it in 07-reference-data.md with sources and "not verified" labels. Do not encode it in the app until confirmed.
  • Consequences: The app cannot mislead users with unverified rarity claims.
